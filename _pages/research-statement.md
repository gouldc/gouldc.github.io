## Academic Background

I began studying household air pollution and the potential for cleaner cooking to improve well-being around the world while an undergraduate at Yale University where I worked as a research assistant for three years with Dr. Rob Bailis. In Honduras, I surveyed households to better understand household energy use patterns, especially with respect to their use of firewood for cooking.

After college, I worked for the Berkeley Air Monitoring Group assessing the sustained use of improved cookstoves in Peru and in Honduras measuring the impacts of improved biomass-burning stoves on household air pollution.

While at Columbia University working towards my PhD in Environmental Health Sciences, I worked on a range of projects, including assessments of the association between asthma symptoms and emergency department visits among adolescents in New York City, the impacts of NYC's Clean Heat Plan on air quality in apartments in northern Manhattan, the determinants of clean cooking fuel adoption and use in rural India, the impacts of clean cookstove interventions on air pollution exposure, birth outcomes, and early child health outcomes in a large randomized controlled trial in rural Ghana, and analysis of cooking fuel use and air pollution exposure patterns in Ecuador.

At Stanford, as a part of the Environmental Change and Human Outcomes Lab, I worked on understanding the health and climate benefits of clean-to-cleaner fuel transitions using Ecuador's ongoing promotion of induction stoves to replace gas cooking and also the health impacts of wildfire smoke pollution and coincident elevated temperatures in the US.

I joined the faculty of UCSD in Summer 2023, and since then have focused on examining the health effects of environmental exposures, teaching at HWSPH and UCSD, and developing interdisciplinary collaborations across UCSD.

## Research Areas

I use a variety of approaches to study a range of environmental health problems. I aim to understand what determines personal environmental exposures, how they impact health, and to what extent we can reduce exposures to lead to healthier lives.

**Adaptation to climate change to improve public health.** Adaptation to climate change is urgently needed. My research tries to understand which programs, policies, and strategies can reduce the impacts of climate hazards on public health.

**Overlap and synergistic health impacts of wildfires and heat.** Wildfires are a substantial and growing source of air pollution in the western US, and will increasingly coincide with elevated temperatures.

**Household transitions to clean cooking fuels.** Displacing polluting cooking fuels with clean-burning fuels promises improved health and well-being. My research examines under what circumstances these transitions can take place in India, Ecuador, and elsewhere.
